\section{Viterbi \& Viterbi}
Figure \ref{fig:viterbi_viterbi} shows the operation of the Viterbi-Viterbi algorithm. A block of symbols \(\left[z[k-N], z[k-N+1],\dots, z[k], \dots, z[k+N]\right]\) is raised to the
4th power to remove data dependency and convolved with a maximum-likelihood (ML) filter of length \(L = 2N + 1\) to compute \(\hat{\theta}_\mathrm{ML}[k]\).
A phase unwrapper is applied to reflect the unbounded Wiener phase noise, then the unwrapped phase correction is applied
to \(z[k]\). Here \(M = 4\) for QAM.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{viterbi_viterbi.png}
    \caption{Viterbi \& Viterbi algorithm block diagram}
    \label{fig:viterbi_viterbi}
\end{figure}
An energy efficient hardware implementation of this algorithm avoids multipliers where possible. The ML filter is known beforehand
so the multiplications can be replaced with fixed additions and shifts. Computing the argument of the filter output
and the phase correction can be implemented with CORDIC. The unwrapped phase is computed as
\[\hat{\theta}_\mathrm{PU}[k] = \hat{\theta}_\mathrm{ML}[k] + n[k]\frac{\pi}{2},\]
where \(n[k]\ \in \{-1, 0, 1\}\) is calcuated as  
\[n[k] = \left\lfloor\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\hat{\theta}_\mathrm{PU}[k-1] - \hat{\theta}_\mathrm{ML}[k]}{\pi/2}\right\rfloor.\]
The floor operation can be implemented efficiently with comparators (negligible cost) \\and division by \(\pi/2\) can be implemented as
multiplication by \(2/\pi\) with fixed shifts and additions as before. However the 4th power operation requires
complex multiplications which increase the energy cost. Assuming Gauss's algorithm for complex multiplication,
the total cost of this algorithm per symbol in terms of real multiplications (RM), real additions (RA), and bit-shifts
(BS) for \(n\)-bit fixed point precision with \(N_{C}\) CORDIC iterations is shown in Table \ref{tab:viterbi_viterbi_cost}. Note that the number
of shifts and adds for multiplication by a fixed value would be \(h\) and \(h-1\), where \(h\) is the Hamming weight, but here it is approximated as \(n\) for simplicity.
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        Operation & RM & RA & BS \\
        \midrule
        4th Power & \(9L\) & \(15L\) & 0 \\
        ML Filter & 0 & \(2(n-1)L\) & \(nL\) \\
        ML Phase & 0 & \(3N_C\) & \(2N_C\) \\
        Phase Unwrapper & 0 & \(3 + 2(n-1)\) & \(2n\) \\
        Phase Correction & 0 & \(3N_C\) & \(2N_C\) \\
        Total & 9L & \(2nL + 13L + 6N_C + 2n + 1\) & \(nL + 4N_C + 2n\) \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Energy cost of Viterbi-Viterbi algorithm per symbol}
    \label{tab:viterbi_viterbi_cost}
\end{table}

\section{Decision-Directed}
The Decision-Directed algorithm avoids the 4th power operation by using a decision circuit to estimate transmitted
symbols \(\hat{s}[k]\). Previous estimates are then used to compute the phase correction as shown in Figure \ref{fig:decision_directed}.
Apart from the decision circuit, it is clear that any complex multiplcations can be avoided by using CORDIC. The ML phase
is computed as \[\hat{\theta}_\mathrm{ML}[k] = \arg\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L} w_{ML}[i]z[k-i]\hat{s}^*[k-i]\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{L}w_{ML}[i]\{\arg(z[k-i]) - \arg(\hat{s}[k-i])\}\]
which requires \(2L\) CORDIC operations as well as shifts and additions for the ML filter.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{decision_directed.png}
    \caption{Decision-Directed algorithm block diagram}
    \label{fig:decision_directed}
\end{figure}
The decision circuit must find the constellation symbol with minimum squared distance to the corrected symbol \(v[k]\). This is calculated as
\[d_m = \min_{s \in \mathcal{S}} |v[k] - \hat{s}[k]|^2\]
where \(\mathcal{S}\) is the set of constellation symbols. This appears to require complex multiplications. However, in reality these distances are small and do not require
high precision to decide correctly so a look-up table (LUT) can be used. The total cost of this algorithm is shown
in Table \ref{tab:decision_directed_cost}. Here LUT accesses are approximated to cost the same as a real addition for simplicity.
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        Operation & RM & RA & BS \\
        \midrule
        ML Filter + Phase & 0 & \(6LN_C + (n-1)L + 2L - 1\) & \(nL + 4LN_C\) \\
        Phase Correction & 0 & \(3N_C\) & \(2N_C\) \\
        Decision Circuit & 0 & \(|\mathcal{S} |\) & 0 \\
        Total & 0 & \(nL + 6LN_C + L + 3N_C + |\mathcal{S}| - 1\) & \(nL + 4LN_C + 2N_C\) \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Energy cost of Decision-Directed algorithm per symbol}
    \label{tab:decision_directed_cost}
\end{table}

\section{Computational Cost}
As each stage of CORDIC applies a left bit-shift, the accuracy achieved by \(N_C\) iterations is approximately \(N_C\) bits.
Thus setting sensible values of \(N_C = n = 8\), \(L = 5\), and \(|\mathcal{S}| = 16\) for 16-QAM, the total cost of each algorithm is shown in Table \ref{tab:cost_comparison}.
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        Algorithm & RM & RA & BS \\
        \midrule
        Viterbi-Viterbi & 45 & 210 & 88 \\
        Decision-Directed & 0 & 324 & 216 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Energy cost comparison of Viterbi \& Viterbi and Decision-Directed algorithms per symbol}
    \label{tab:cost_comparison}
\end{table}
The exact energy cost of each operation depends on how adders and multipliers are implemented, but the Decision-Directed
algorithm is likely to incur a lower energy cost.
\section{Block-Based Estimation}
Both the Viterbi \& Viterbi and Decision-Directed algorithms can be implemented in a block-based manner to
reduce the energy cost. Instead of computing a phase estimate and correcting for every symbol, the estimate for one
symbol can be applied to a block of \(B\) symbols. This reduces the cost of the ML filter and phase correction by a factor of \(B\)
at the cost of increased phase noise variance.
